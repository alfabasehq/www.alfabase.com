The shared foundation for business software

One ERP engine. Any application.

Build the business software you want, without rebuilding the ERP underneath it.

Alfabase is a headless ERP that gives custom applications and AI agents a common data model, transaction engine and accounting foundation. Build your own applications. Use applications built by others. Let them all work with the same underlying business records.

01Building software is getting easier. Making it work together is not.

AI is changing how software gets built. Applications that once required months of development can increasingly be created in days or hours. Businesses can build tools around their exact workflows instead of adapting to somebody else’s software.

But this creates a new problem. Every application needs to represent the same business concepts: customers, products, orders, invoices, payments and accounts. When every developer creates their own data model, every application becomes an island.

An inventory app does not automatically understand a sales app. An invoicing app uses different records from an accounting app. Connecting them means writing integrations, mapping data, and keeping everything synchronised.

AI can make building individual applications cheap. It does not automatically make independently built applications compatible. The next generation of business software needs more than easier ways to build applications. It needs a common foundation.

02One foundation. A world of applications.

What if business applications did not have to reinvent the ERP underneath them? That is the idea behind Alfabase. It provides a shared ERP engine that applications can build upon instead of creating their own incompatible business systems.

Alfabase defines how business records are represented, how transactions behave, and how the accounting stays consistent. Applications built for Alfabase work with the same underlying model. A sales application knows what an invoice is. An inventory application works with the same products and orders. An accounting application reads from the same financial foundation.

Each application can have its own interface, features and workflow. They do not have to invent their own version of the business.

Different applications. One business system.

03The ERP underneath everything

A shared foundation is only useful if it can represent the business activities applications depend on. The planned foundation spans 42 modules across six areas of business operations, rather than a minimal ledger that leaves every developer to fill in the gaps.

Sales and receivables

Quotations, sales orders, billing, invoicing, customer balances, recurring billing and collections reporting.

Purchasing and payables

Purchase orders, supplier invoices, expenses, credit notes, remittances and supplier balances.

Inventory, assets and operations

Stock management, manufacturing, delivery notes, fixed assets, payroll and service billing.

Cash and accounting

General ledger, chart of accounts, journal entries, bank reconciliation, cash management and multi-currency.

Business structure and rules

Tax handling, accounting methods, departmental and project accounting, custom fields and configuration.

Financial reporting

Profit and loss, balance sheet, trial balance, equity changes, comparative reporting and transaction drill-down.

04From the developer of Manager.io

Alfabase is being built by the developer of Manager.io, accounting software used by more than a million businesses. It covers invoicing, inventory, payroll, tax, multi-currency and financial reporting.

The hard part of an ERP engine is not the API. It is getting the accounting and the business rules underneath it right, across every module, and keeping them right. That is work we have done before.

Alfabase is not Manager.io with the interface removed. It is a new engine, written from scratch to be headless from the first line. Its data model is informed by what building Manager.io taught us about how business records actually behave.

A new engine. Not a first attempt.
